Executive Summary

The Okapi Wildlife Reserve continues to face significant pressure from multi-vector environmental threats. While primary forest cover remains largely intact in the core zone, the expansion of artisanal mining sites and illegal logging infrastructure in the buffer zones represents a critical challenge for conservation management.

Our 2026 assessment detects a 12% acceleration in forest fragmentation compared to the 2018-2022 baseline, primarily driven by road expansion associated with mining permits in the Epulu and Mambasa sectors.

Key Findings

  • 01

    Mining Encroachment

    124 active mining sites mapped within 50km of the reserve boundary, with 12 sites showing active encroachment inside protected zones.

  • 02

    Fire Recurrence

    4,250 ha impacted by fire anomalies in the current cycle, predominantly associated with small-scale agricultural clearing at the reserve frontiers.

  • 03

    Carbon Sequestration

    Estimated cumulative carbon loss of 1.8M tons CO2e since 2000, representing a significant loss in ecosystem service value.

Recommendations

Immediate Action

Increase patrol frequency in high-risk mining corridors and implement a 5km no-extraction buffer zone strictly enforced via satellite telemetry.

Strategic Policy

Formalize artisanal mining cooperatives outside the reserve boundary and integrate local communities into the carbon credit benefit-sharing mechanism.
